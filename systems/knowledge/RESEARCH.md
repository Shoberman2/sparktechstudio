# Recursive Self-Enterprise Improvement: research and build map

Research checked September 10, 2026. Recommendations below are engineering judgments based on the linked primary sources, not claims that these integrations are already installed or effective for these companies.

The strongest product is a reusable learning-and-action system with separate company control planes. Its compounding asset is evidence about what worked, where, and under what constraints. A podcast summary alone does not create that asset. SparkTech can own the reusable software; UTern remains an independent consumer. LEED, Ballot Watch and future associated companies are intended users, not live integrations.

## How the knowledge product should work

1. A founder supplies an authorized source and a learning intent, such as improving onboarding. Record source identity, publication date, retrieval date, content hash and rights attestation. Preserve original text; captions and speaker names can be wrong.
2. Extract exact passages and candidate techniques. Store the speaker's claim separately from the analyst's interpretation, independent corroboration and measured company outcomes. A matching quotation proves attribution, not truth.
3. Match to a company's current goal and constraints. Ask whether the mechanism is plausible here, prerequisites exist, implementation is reversible, and measurement is possible. Explicitly retain reasons to reject advice.
4. Draft a bounded experiment: owner, hypothesis, target population, metric/denominator, baseline, duration, budget, success rule, guardrail and rollback. Review before handing it to an execution system. Podcast text never becomes a command or policy.
5. Link execution evidence and outcome back to the proposal. Preserve inconclusive and negative outcomes. Promote a narrowly worded lesson only after review; company observations do not become universal facts.
6. On source corrections, expired review dates, contradictory evidence or changed company goals, reassess relevant claims and approvals. Start with explicit links and review deadlines; later automate change detection and suggested conflict pairs.

Imported media can contain indirect prompt injection. Keeping external text out of tool authority is an architectural requirement, not a promise that a prompt will always resist manipulation. [OWASP prompt injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)

## Build choices

**Start with a relational evidence ledger.** Exact IDs and spans are easier to audit than a vector-only memory store. SQLite is sufficient for the local single-operator prototype. A production service needs authenticated tenant authorization and stronger audit/transaction guarantees. Add full-text retrieval first; evaluate embeddings only when terminology mismatch measurably hurts retrieval. Retrieval rank must never imply factual confidence.

**Buy extraction capacity, own the decision record.** A model can propose structured interpretations using source spans in a tool-free call. The system should reject fabricated spans, missing applicability, and incomplete experiment bounds. Hosted models reduce operational effort but require company-specific approval for data transfer and budget; local models reduce external transfer but need hardware and quality evaluation. No provider credentials or transcript connector was exercised for this slice, so extraction quality is not yet demonstrated.

**Share reviewed lessons, not tenant memory.** Export separately authored approved general text with an opaque provenance token. Keep the originating private evidence local. A receiving company makes its own applicability decision. Human disclosure review is still required; removing a company name is not reliable anonymization.

**Keep one execution foundation.** The shared QA runner owns subprocesses, isolation, budgets, verification and recovery. The knowledge ledger emits a reviewed request and later references runner company ID, run ID, candidate digest and evidence. The current QA stage model is not a generic business-experiment executor; build a deliberate adapter when a real experiment is selected.

## Prioritized sequence and acceptance gates

1. **Now: evidence ledger and controlled demonstration.** Delivered local intake, exact passages, human-reviewed claims, proposals, approval checks, outcomes, general-lesson exports and stale/conflict blocking. Gate: tests pass, fixture labels visible, real authorized text can enter without modifying code.
2. **Next: one founder-selected real transcript and one company goal.** Select source permission, owner, pilot company, review budget and success metric. Produce at most three experiments. Gate: reviewer confirms source fidelity and at least one useful, feasible proposal. No employee viewing surveillance is needed.
3. **Then: extraction evaluation and shared-runner adapter.** Build a small adjudicated corpus, test a tool-free model, add authenticated review and explicit execution mapping. Gate: no invalid evidence references, acceptable semantic fidelity and rejection behavior, tenant-bound permissions and rollback verified. Choose quality thresholds from pilot results before increasing volume.
4. **Then: observability and support intake.** Reuse the evidence model for authorized telemetry/ticket signals. Gate: reproducible diagnosis with useful prioritization, tested recovery, and no unintended data sharing.
5. **Then: measured product experiments.** Add reliable event definitions and feature-flag adapters only where traffic and consent permit useful measurement. Gate: trustworthy exposure/denominator data and a pre-registered evaluation plan.
6. **Later: controlled workflow improvement and social learning.** Evaluate workflow changes continuously against withheld cases. Resume social work only after the user's naming/account decision. Gate: measured benefit after costs and human review, without increased failure or unauthorized action rates.

Continuous improvement should be a repeatable, scheduled cycle only after these gates are demonstrated. The current deliverable contains no recurring automation. The first business question is whether this system creates better decisions per hour of founder review; autonomous volume comes later.
